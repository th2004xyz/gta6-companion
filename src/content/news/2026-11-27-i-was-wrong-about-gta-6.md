## Overview

Some months ago, I wrote about how, even prior to its full reveal, we already knew what GTA 6 was going to be . Although not unique to the franchise by any means, GTA games had always followed a very clear formula, rarely upending it in any meaningful way. I strongly suspected that the 13-year wait for GTA 6 would result in a very pretty version of a very familiar game. As release approached, I worried that for all of the praise heaped upon its story and incredible level of detail, all of that was merely a veneer on the same game we've been playing for 25 years. And in many ways, that is true; at its most basic level, GTA 6 is still a game about driving from point A to point B, and maybe shooting some ne'er-do-wells when you get there. But I dramatically underestimated the impact of that veneer, and just how differently a familiar formula can feel when accompanied by impactful systems and relationships. The freeform nature of GTA 6's world, and the objectives you have over the course of a given mission, are largely identical to previous games. You can just drive around and make your own fun, or deal with violent drug smugglers, or pilot a boat or plane or car through a series of checkpoints. It's all very familiar on paper. But never before in a GTA game have I considered whether I have time to go and do one of those things because I have someone I care about waiting at home. Only rarely in a GTA have I decided to drive carefully, lest I face the consequences of doing almost anything I'd traditionally associate with a GTA game. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c38Opkxzqvc So much of GTA 6's focus is on the relationship between Lucia and Jason. I quickly found myself caring about them both, and for all of Rockstar's talk of their romantic relationship being optional, I truly can't fathom playing this in a way that doesn't fully embrace their love. And the way that permeates things even in Chapter 1--the phone calls, the text messages, the interactions at home--has forced some degree of role-playing on me. I check the time on my in-game phone not because I'm wondering if it's late enough to meet a contact in the woods, but because I want to be sure I'm home in time to hang out, whether that's talking, watching TV, or browsing our respective phones together. Despite the insistence of your boss, Brian, that you get down to a meeting spot for your next mission, I delayed doing so because it was a long drive late in the day, and the thought of leaving my partner hanging was unacceptable. Were that the only thing I was doing differently in GTA 6 than in past games, I think it would still fall into the category of veneer--a bit of narrative-laced framing that surrounds the usual chaos of going out and driving down sidewalks and blowing up cars. Instead, that more thoughtful approach to things extends to almost all of the moment-to-moment gameplay. I regularly drive the speed limit (something made much easier by the fantastic usage of the PS5's DualSense triggers ). I tend to stay on the road and in my lane, and well away from any pedestrians or other cars. I do frequently blow through red lights, but only after looking both ways. When I pull off a robbery, I tend to target other criminals, rather than stores. I routinely pull over when I need to use my phone while driving. I don't hijack random cars driving by--some 25 hours in, I've not done this most basic of actions in a GTA game even once. A small amount of this does stem from that urge to role-play as these characters. Jason and Lucia might be criminals, but they also feel like real people, and it's hard to imagine them running over innocent bystanders or smashing up their cars because you're intent on reaching your destination in as little time as possible.
